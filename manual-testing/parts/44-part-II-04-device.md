### 4.1 📱 From this device

**Screen:** Operate → **Run diagnostic** · **Routes:** `GET /dnssite/list` (`dns_site_view`), `POST /diagnostic/clientobservation` (`diagnostic_run`, licensed), and `GET /organisation/settings` for the test with no ticket; that test's target is set with `PUT /organisation/settings` (`staff_manage`), not on this screen

- 🌟 **Commercial Presentation & Sales Pitch**: "Is it slow for me?" answered from the customer's own connection, to the customer's own endpoint: the engineer opens the page and the endpoint nearest them is already chosen, from the position entered for each endpoint. It shows the device's IPv4 **and** IPv6 addresses, then files the run against the ticket, under the endpoint it measured — labelled as measured on a device.
- 📖 **User Guide & Operational Flow**: **Run diagnostic** is the test from this device, filed against a ticket (since 2026-10-07 the page no longer sweeps from the server). The ticket comes first: **Customer ID**, **TT number**, optional **Notes** and the **Endpoint**; **Start test** does nothing until the customer, the TT number and the endpoint are given, and marks the one missing. As the page opens the device is asked where it is, and the endpoint nearest it — by the latitude and longitude entered under DNS sites (§3.1) — is chosen, with *Nearest to this device, N km away*. An engineer is given that endpoint and cannot change it (*Chosen for you: the endpoint nearest where this device is.*). Whoever manages the endpoints (`dns_site_manage`) may pick another, and go back with **Choose by my location**. With no position to go by — location refused or unknown, or no endpoint placed — everyone picks by hand, and the page says why. Choosing another endpoint empties a finished run, and stops one under way. The position chooses; it is filed with a run only for people whose location is recorded (§2.1). Someone who may not run diagnostics, or has no organisation, gets the device test alone, with no ticket, against the organisation's internal host or Cloudflare DNS, which the screen does not name. The line-speed test is hidden. On the public demo the **Customer ID** and **TT number** open filled in with the example the fields show, `CUST-88412` and `TT-2026-00731`, so a walkthrough goes straight to **Start test**; they can be changed, and what is in them is what is filed (since 2026-10-09).
  - Above the controls, three cards: the **IPv4** address, the **IPv6** address (or *No IPv6 connectivity*) and the **Nearest edge** that answered, with an *IPv6 active* or *IPv4 only* badge. No network operator or AS number is shown: finding one would send the engineer's address to a third party.
  - In a browser the test is **UDP, like a call** by default (§4.2), or **HTTPS to the endpoint**: an HTTPS request to its address, timed end to end. The app on a desktop or phone sends a real ICMP echo where the device allows it, and offers no UDP test. An endpoint that does not serve HTTPS — most routers and resolvers do not — shows every sample unanswered in a browser, even when it is up, and the note beside the figures says so.
  - Below the controls, the route says what the test goes to (since 2026-10-09): *Start will test*, then *Testing now*, then *This run tested*, over *This device → method → destination*. With a ticket it is marked **Path to the endpoint** (green, a tick) or **Not the endpoint** (amber, a warning sign) and says why in one sentence: *HTTPS requests from this device to Pune Edge…*, *A real ping from this device to Pune Edge…*, or, for the UDP test, the reflector beside the endpoint or the PacketPulse network (§4.2). With no ticket it reads *The test target*, naming no host, and carries no mark.
  - **IP version** — *Auto*, *IPv4 only*, *IPv6 only* — holds a run against the default target to one address family, to compare the two paths. It is offered only on the test with no ticket, when the organisation tests the default over HTTPS: an endpoint is reached at its own address, a browser cannot choose the family a hostname resolves to, and the native ping is IPv4.
  - While a run goes, eight figures fill in — average, fastest, slowest, jitter, standard deviation, loss, MOS and progress — with **Round-trip, sample by sample** (jitter as a band, the average dashed, a red mark per unanswered sample; point at it to read one), **Round-trips by band** (under 20, 20–50, 50–100, 100–200, 200 ms and over) and a **Probe log** of every reply. **Clear results** empties the figures and charts and keeps the log; **Clear log** empties the log.

### 4.2 📶 UDP test and reflectors

**Pages:** Operate → **Run diagnostic**, in a browser; `/selftest.html` → **PacketPulse network — UDP, like a call** · **Routes:** `POST /reflector/session` (public, limited per address), `POST /diagnostic/endpoint/{dnsSiteId}/webrtc-offer` (`diagnostic_run`, licensed), `POST /diagnostic/endpoint/{dnsSiteId}/reflector-key` (`dns_site_manage`, licensed, audited)

- 🌟 **Commercial Presentation & Sales Pitch**: The only browser test that sees real packet loss, and says which way it was lost. A customer opens a page, and in 30 seconds the engineer knows whether the customer's uplink drops packets, their downlink does, or packets arrive too late to play — the three causes of a choppy call, told apart from where the customer sits.
- 📖 **User Guide & Operational Flow**: On the self-test, **Target → PacketPulse network — UDP, like a call** replaces Samples, Interval and Timeout with **Duration** (10, 30 or 60 seconds) and sends 50 packets a second, 200 bytes each, to PacketPulse's own reflector. The tiles read **UDP round-trip**, **Jitter** with *Up … ms · down … ms* under it, and **Packet loss** with *Up …% · down …% · late …%*. Loss is counted at the end, per packet, when the reflector says which packets reached it; until then the loss tile shows a dash. A packet back more than a second after it was sent is *late*, and counts in the loss figure and the MOS, because a call would have played silence. *Not sent (device busy)* counts slots the browser could not keep; they are left out of every percentage. Keep the tab in front: a hidden tab stops the test.
  - When UDP cannot get through, the page says *UDP is blocked on this network* and points at the DNS-over-HTTPS test, which still works. It never shows that as 100% loss.
  - A customer's own reflector (`packetpulsereflector`, on a PoP, an SBC's neighbour or a VPS) is installed with its endpoint's key: `POST /diagnostic/endpoint/{dnsSiteId}/reflector-key` returns the key and the install line, `sudo ./install-reflector.sh --key <key> [--public-ip <address>]`. Sessions on it are then opened only through `POST /diagnostic/endpoint/{dnsSiteId}/webrtc-offer`, which signs the request and checks that the answer is signed back.
  - **In Run diagnostic** (since 2026-10-09), in a browser with a ticket, the test is **UDP, like a call** by default, with **Test length** 10, 30 or 60 seconds in place of the HTTPS steppers. It is set up through the signed-in relay, so the person's session caps it, not the office's address. It measures to the reflector beside the ticket's endpoint when the endpoint has a **UDP reflector address** (§3.1), and otherwise to the PacketPulse network. The route below the controls (§4.1) says which before **Start test**, while the test runs and after it: *UDP over WebRTC → The reflector beside Pune Edge*, **Path to the endpoint**; or *UDP over WebRTC → The PacketPulse network*, **Not the endpoint**, in amber, with *Pune Edge has no UDP reflector, so this test goes to the PacketPulse network instead…* — or *…rather than to Pune Edge…* when the PacketPulse network was chosen after the endpoint's reflector failed, or when the endpoint's reflector address is PacketPulse's own. The route follows the server: once it answers, the route shows the reflector that answered, and the probe log says *Answered by the PacketPulse network, not Pune Edge* or *Answered by the reflector beside Pune Edge*. *What this run found* says which: *The reflector beside Pune Edge*, or *The PacketPulse network: the customer's access line, not the path to the endpoint* — and then no endpoint line. **Lost up · down** and *n late* sit with the figures; *Where packets went* reads, for example, *Upstream lost 4 (0.3%); Downstream lost 2 (0.1%); Late 4; Jitter up 9.4 ms, down 4.6 ms*. **Attach to a ticket** files it with the split; the ticket's page and PDF print the same line. A test to the PacketPulse network is filed as *PacketPulse network* at PacketPulse's address, never under the endpoint.
  - **Progress** counts the run's packet slots against what the reflector allows, not against what was asked for: *Testing 383/1500* and *Packet 383 of 1500* while it runs. A slot this device was too busy to keep is skipped, not sent late, and still counts, so a finished run reads **100%** with *1492 sent · 8 skipped* (since 2026-10-09; it used to stop at *99%*). Under the figures, **What happened** says the run in words: *The test ran its full 30 s, with room for 1500 packets at 50 a second. This device sent 1492. The other 8 were skipped: the device was too busy to send them on time… Skipped packets are not counted as lost, and say nothing about the network. All 1492 came back in time.* A stopped run says *The test was stopped part-way: n of its 1500 packets had their turn.*; a run the reflector's limits cut down adds *That is what the reflector's limits allowed, rather than the 1500 packets asked for.* The log adds *Pacing: 1500 packets, 50 a second* and, when any were skipped, *Skipped: 8 of 1500 packets, this device was busy*.
  - When the endpoint's reflector cannot run the test — down, busy, or installed with another key — the page shows the server's reason and offers **Test to the PacketPulse network instead**. *UDP is blocked on this network* offers **Run the HTTPS test**. A refusal for who the person is (signed out, no permission, no licence) offers nothing else. **Stop** ends sending early; the packets already sent are still waited for, so a stopped run is a shorter run, not a lossy one.

- ⚙️ **Developer Guide & Release Confidence**:
  - The reflector: `packetpulsego/pkg/reflectormicroservice/reflectorcore/ReflectorEngine.go` (pion, ICE-lite, passive DTLS, multicast DNS off, one multiplexed UDP port), `packetpulsego/pkg/reflectormicroservice/reflectorcore/ReflectorSession.go` (the caps and the per-sequence bitmap) and `packetpulsego/pkg/reflectormicroservice/reflectorcore/ReflectorProtocol.go` (the frames). An echo is exactly as long as its probe, so the reflector cannot amplify. Offers declaring `a=ice-lite` are refused: with both ends lite, pion selects a pair without any check and sends to it, and `TestALiteOfferNeverMakesTheReflectorSendToACallerChosenAddress` proves a caller-chosen address hears nothing.
  - Real loss: `TestRealLossIsSplitExactlyByDirection` in `packetpulsego/pkg/reflectormicroservice/reflectorcore/ReflectorLoss_test.go` drops real datagrams through a UDP proxy and checks the bitmap and the echoes against the drops; it also fails if the probe channel ever retransmits.
  - Signalling: `packetpulsego/pkg/reflectormicroservice/reflectorapp/ReflectorRouteHandler.go` (per-address limit, signatures, replay refusal, the signed answer) with `packetpulsego/pkg/reflectormicroservice/reflectorsign/ReflectorSign.go` (keys derived from `REFLECTOR_KEY_SECRET`, never stored). The hosted reflector: `packetpulsego/cmd/packetpulseserver/PacketPulseReflector.go`. The customer daemon: `packetpulsego/cmd/packetpulsereflector/main.go`, `scripts/reflector/install-reflector.sh`, `scripts/reflector/packetpulsereflector.service`.
  - The relay: `packetpulsego/pkg/diagnosticmicroservice/diagnosticservice/DiagnosticReflectorRelay.go`, through the sweep's probe policy and the demo's public-only confinement; tenancy, policy and every reflector failure in `packetpulsego/pkg/diagnosticmicroservice/diagnosticservice/DiagnosticReflectorRelay_test.go`. An endpoint whose reflector address is PacketPulse's own (`REFLECTOR_PUBLIC_IP`, or a name resolving to it, any port) is answered by the hosted reflector in-process and named the PacketPulse network: the relay would otherwise post to TCP 50000 on this host, where nothing listens (`namesHostedReflector`, `TestAnEndpointNamingPacketPulsesReflectorIsTestedToIt`).
  - In the app: the engine is the self-test's own, copied to `packetpulseflutter/web/engine/` (`packetpulseflutter/test/udp_probe_run_test.dart` fails if either copy differs from `packetpulseweb/assets/`). `packetpulseflutter/lib/diagnosticmicroservice/service/udp/UdpProbeRun.dart` maps its result to samples and the split, checked against `packetpulsetest/contracts/udp_run_fixture.json`, which the engine's own test regenerates; `UdpProbeRunnerWeb.dart` is the browser bridge, `UdpProbeRunnerNative.dart` says no. The CSV export's columns are unchanged. Signed in, the hosted reflector counts sessions per person (`user:<id>`), not per address.
